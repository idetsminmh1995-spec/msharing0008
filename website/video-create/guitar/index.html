3Guitar Fretboard
The neck lights where each note is played — its string, its fret and the finger stopping it, with the fret numbers along the bottom. When the score writes a position or a fingering down, that is what you see; when it does not, the Human Finger Engine works out how a guitarist would play the line: it weighs every place each note could go, what the hand would have to stretch, and how long it has to get there, then picks the way through with the least work in it. The picking hand is drawn too, as the square bracket and the V every guitarist reads. Pick the guitar and it brings its own neck with it — a classical stops at 19 frets with nothing in its board, a dreadnought around 20, an electric at 22, a modern extended-range at 24 — so there is no fret count to set: that is a fact about the instrument, not a view. Six strings throughout. The Acoustic is a PHOTOGRAPH of a real cutaway dreadnought, measured fret by fret so every mark still lands on the right string in the right fret; the others are drawn out of shapes and gradients, headstock, tuners, wood and all, at whatever size you export. Then say what the right hand is doing: a plectrum is written as the square bracket and the V every guitarist reads, and fingers are written a letter at a time on the string each one takes — p the thumb, then i, m and a. A slide travels along the string rather than jumping, an open string has its own colour, and the four finger colours are fixed, not chosen: they are meant to be learnt once and read in every video after.
